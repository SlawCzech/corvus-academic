# Władysław Kargul — expected findings

MA in article form, average with a methodological trap. Rubric:
`master_article.md` (UE Katowice, Zarządzenie 117/22 zał. 2).
Body ~55 000 chars (suggested 55 000–65 000, lists and appendices excluded);
whole document ~67 000. Primary target of `evaluate_methodology`.

**Formally complete on purpose.** Abstract 129 words with aim, method, results
and conclusion; five keywords; introduction with problem, aims, H1–H3, all four
scopes, methods, structure; literature review; methodology; results; discussion
that argues with earlier studies; conclusions with hypotheses, limitations,
recommendations and further research; 49 scholarly items (far more than 8 in
foreign languages), all cited in the text, APA, alphabetical and numbered;
appendices A–C. Well written, theory is sound.

So **no CRITICAL or MAJOR formal finding is expected.** A review that scores it
high because it is tidy and well-read has walked into the trap; the method does
not carry the conclusions.

## The trap: what a good review must say

1. **One case, conclusions about a market.** A single boundary dispute between
   two households (0.03 ha) is generalised to "polski rynek ziemi rolnej" and
   "polskie rolnictwo indywidualne" (abstract, H3, 4.1, 4.4, conclusions).
2. **"Critical case" is asserted, not shown.** Flyvbjerg's logic needs a case
   where the theory is *most* likely to hold. Two families with a feud going
   back before 1945 — who chose each other as neighbours — are the least likely
   pair to bargain; the case was in effect selected on the outcome. The thesis
   itself reports (4.3) that a dozen other boundary discrepancies in the same
   village were settled quietly: the comparison cases exist and contradict the
   generalisation.
3. **The Coase theorem is "refuted" under conditions it does not cover.** The
   theorem is conditional on zero transaction costs and well-defined rights. The
   thesis measures transaction costs at 23× the stake, and the boundary was
   undefined until 2018 — the result *illustrates* Coase (1988, cited by the
   author) rather than contradicting him. 4.1 gets there by equating
   transaction costs with the few hours spent talking, against the author's own
   broader definition (Allen, 2.4).
4. **No evidence that the final allocation is inefficient.** No trade after the
   ruling is read as "the law decides", but it is equally what the theorem
   predicts if the winner values the strip more. Who values it more is never
   established — although 3.4 hints B needs it for access and A stores firewood
   there. The thesis has the material for the real test and does not use it.
5. **One-sided data.** The author belongs to household A; three of seven
   interviewees are his family; household B refused and is reconstructed from
   court pleadings. "Causes of failed negotiation" (table 2) are therefore one
   party's account, coded by the author alone.
6. **H3 "confirmed" by counting interviewees** (5–6 of 7 mention informal
   causes) — a tally of opinions in one village, not evidence about barriers on
   a national land market.
7. **Cost figures are softer than they look.** Time cost is a reconstruction
   from memory priced at a wage "according to the interviewees"; B's costs are
   simply assumed equal, which is where "82 tys. zł" and "45×" come from. The
   strip's value uses one average price per hectare.
8. **Endowment-effect reading is a stretch.** A's 5 000 zł ask included, in R1's
   own words, compensation for the past — not a valuation of the land; and B,
   not A, was the party in possession.
9. **Polemic with Ellickson rests on n = 1**, as does the claim that formal
   titling raises transaction costs and the policy conclusion that only
   administrative land consolidation can work (4.4).
10. **Limitations are named but neutralised**: "jeden przypadek, przy czym …
    krytyczny, co pozwala na uogólnienie analityczne".

## What is genuinely good (a fair review says this too)

- H1 and H2 are properly scoped to the case and supported by documents.
- The cost account of a real dispute, with appendix B, is a useful descriptive
  contribution.
- The author discloses his position and separates documented events from
  accounts (2.5), and presents B's position from the files (3.4).
- The observation that the parties litigate *through* the law without accepting
  its rulings as final is interesting — as a hypothesis for further study.
