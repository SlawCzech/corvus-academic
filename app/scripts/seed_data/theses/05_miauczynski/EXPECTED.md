## v1 — what a review should find

CRITICAL (rubric § 4):

1. No discussion section.
2. Abstract states aim and method but neither results nor conclusions.
3. Methodology does not define the scopes (time, space) of the study.
4. Conclusions without limitations.
5. 17 literature items (40 required).
6. Empirical thesis without appendices.

MAJOR:

7. Volume about a third of the suggested 55 000–65 000 chars.
8. Introduction incomplete: no time or spatial scope, no structure worth the name.
9. Literature review covers burnout psychology only; the economics of education —
   the half of the title about "efektywność systemu edukacji" — is absent.

Substance:

10. **n = 1 generalised to a national system.** One teacher's diary "proves"
    that burnout lowers the efficiency of education in Poland.
11. Efficiency is measured as the author's own estimate of the share of his
    lesson plan he covered; "blisko 40% nakładów na nauczanie jest marnowane"
    follows from it.
12. Both variables are same-day self-ratings by one person; the correlation
    (r = –0.52) cannot carry a causal claim.
13. Digressions unrelated to the problem: correct Polish usage ("mieć stresa"),
    neighbours' noise as a research gap; the share of pupils who read the set
    book as a "measure of system efficiency"; the 1–7 scale chosen because
    "liczba siedem jest dla autora wygodna".
14. Recommendation to tighten the law on disturbing the peace.

## v2 — what `check_progress` should report

**Fixed**

- Discussion added, and it does engage earlier studies (Klusmann, Arens & Morin,
  Madigan & Kim, Hanushek, Ingersoll).
- Abstract complete and within 150 words; five keywords.
- Scopes defined (2.2); introduction has aims, H1–H3, methods, structure.
- Literature 44 items, economics of education included (1.4, 1.5).
- Appendices added. Limitations stated. Further research indicated.
- Second data source: survey with a validated instrument (OLBI, Polish
  adaptation), reliability of the own scale reported (α = 0.71).
- The "mieć stresa" digression and the "40% of spending is wasted" claim are gone.

**Partly fixed**

- Volume: body ~44 000 chars, still under the suggested 55 000.
- H1 and H2 are now properly scoped. H3 still claims an effect on the
  efficiency of the education system in Poland, which nothing in the study
  measures. 1.4 itself says efficiency needs data on pupils' results — the
  study has none.
- Limitations are listed and then waved away: "nie podważają jednak głównego
  wniosku".

**Still there**

- The diary (n = 1) remains a main source and "confirms" H1.
- Noise at home: now a whole discussion subsection (4.4) and a recommendation.
- Set-book readership as a measure of teaching effects (3.4).
- The seven-point scale chosen because seven is convenient for the author.

**New problems introduced in v2**

- **Extrapolation to 3.7 bn zł a year** (3.8): the share of exhausted teachers
  among 43 respondents is assumed for all of Poland, a 14% gap in self-rated
  effectiveness is treated as a 14% loss of output, and the wage bill of 60 bn zł
  is an assumption. The figure goes into the abstract and "confirms" H3.
- Convenience sample: two schools, questionnaires handed out and collected by
  the author among his colleagues; the author's own questionnaire is included
  in the 43.
- Common-method bias is cited (Podsakoff et al.) and dismissed because the survey
  "agrees with the diary" — which is itself a self-report.
- Causality asserted against Madigan & Kim on the strength of the diary, where
  exhaustion is rated after work (2.3) — it cannot precede the day's teaching.
- Subgroup comparisons on tiny groups (3 and 8 people).
- Editorial slips: table 7 is announced as "tabela 8"; Bandura (1997) is cited
  in 1.5 and missing from the bibliography.
