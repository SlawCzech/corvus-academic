## Why

Demo starts from an empty roster and whatever PDF happens to be at hand, so it
shows nothing in particular. We need a fixed set of theses, each built to exercise
a specific AI action, with defects planted on purpose so we can see whether the
model catches them.

## Conventions

- **Structure and length follow the seeded guidelines**, otherwise every thesis
  gets a "volume out of range" finding and the planted defects drown in it
  (confirmed 2026-09-30):
  - BA (`bachelor.md`, suggested 80 000–130 000 chars): Anioł and Ochódzki at
    ~80–90k; Kiepski deliberately ~30k — being too short is one of his defects.
  - MA is `master_article` — a thesis in the form of a research article
    (abstract ≤ 150 words, 3–5 keywords, introduction, literature review,
    methodology, results, discussion, conclusions; suggested 55 000–65 000 chars,
    lists and appendices excluded). Not chapters. The rubric also demands at
    least 40 literature items, 8 of them foreign-language, and appendices with
    the data in an empirical thesis — each a CRITICAL finding when missing. Formal defects per student: Kargul — formally complete (his
    problem is the method only); Miauczyński v1 — no discussion section, v2
    adds it; Adamczewski — ~250-word abstract and 8 keywords.
- **Templates.** The university's own: `app/scripts/seed_data/thesis_templates/`
  (Zarządzenie 117/22 zał. 3 — BA, zał. 4 — MA article). Title page needs an
  English title, album number, field of study and supervisor; the BA template
  prescribes APA references. Supervisor on every title page: prof. dr hab. Jan Serce.
- **Format.** Markdown source, DOCX generated by a script on top of the
  university template — reproducible and diffable. PDFs are converted by hand
  from the DOCX when needed.
- **Bibliography.** Real, well-known works (Williamson, North, Acemoglu & Robinson,
  Kula…) mixed with openly fictional empirical data. Never invented references that
  look real. A "bad bibliography" is bad in quality (blogs only, nothing recent),
  not fabricated.
- **Expected findings.** Each thesis gets an `EXPECTED.md` listing its planted
  defects — a demo checklist now, a simple eval later.
- **Guidelines.** Theses align with what `seed_guidelines` seeds, and at least one
  (Kiepski) breaks a concrete requirement so RAG has something to find.
- **Students.** Theses hang off seeded students with meeting notes, so the demo
  opens on a populated roster. Plus Kazimierz Pawlak: meeting notes only, no
  thesis — counterpart to Kargul and the `propose_topic` case.

## Seeding

`make seed-demo PROMOTOR=<e-mail>` (`app/scripts/seed_demo.py`) gives an existing
promoter account all seven students. Each fixture directory has a `student.toml`:
the student's details, topic, and a dated timeline of meeting notes and uploaded
versions. The script goes through `StudentService` and `DraftService`, so uploads
are extracted and versioned exactly as in the UI. Students already on the roster
(by name) are skipped. `--dry-run` only reads the data and extracts the files,
and prints each student's timeline with its dates.

Everything is dated from the timeline: the student and the topic from its
first entry, each note and each version from its own. An entry with
`accepted = true` accepts the latest version as final on that day (Anioł).

`AS_OF=today` (`--as-of`) moves the whole timeline by whole weeks so that it
ends in the week before that day. Off by default, because the theses carry
their own dates — Anioł's survey is "w listopadzie 2025 r.", Miauczyński's
diary runs from September 2025 — and a moved timeline puts the first meeting
after the work it was supposed to plan.

The app image copies `scripts/` at build time, so rebuild it (`make build`)
after changing a fixture.
